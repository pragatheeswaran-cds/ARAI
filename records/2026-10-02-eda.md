Worlds: fixtures 2001-2003 (1,000 customers), training 2101-2110 (10,000 customers) and stress 2501-2507.

- **Generation targets are met.** Claim, temporary-use and joint propensity are 4.0%, 3.1% and 0.6%; mean
  action rate 0.198; genuine fulfilment failures 1.5% of delivered units; carrier damage 0.5%. Two measures sit
  below target for a known reason: late honest returns near the cutoff fall after it, and Dormant accounts
  cannot receive the heavy-return overlay.
- **The return-rate rule is inside its 20-30% calibration band** without tuning.
- **Turning a propensity into an action depends on order count.** Among accounts with a propensity, 10% of those
  with no delivered order have acted, 54% of those with 3-5 orders and 92% of those with 21 or more.
- **Shortcuts.** On held-out training worlds, persona alone gives an AUC of 0.65 over all customers and 0.53
  among eligible customers; order count alone gives 0.73 and 0.57. The all-customer figures exceed the 0.60
  level at which the specification asks for an investigation: the cause is the 30% of accounts with no order,
  who can never be abusers. Among eligible customers neither shortcut is strong.
- **Evaluation segments are large enough.** Over five training worlds the benign segments hold 2,308 heavy
  returners, 5,207 defective-product buyers and 3,222 household members, against the 200 the gate requires.
- **What the two rules would do to the gate's segments.** The return-rate rule wrongly selects 44.6% of honest
  heavy returners, 6.1% of defective-product buyers and 4.2% of household members. The claim-count rule selects
  5.2%, 7.6% and 3.0%: it would fail the defective-buyer leg. A candidate model has to beat 4.2% on households
  and 6.1% on defective-product buyers, which are the tight legs.
- **Stress worlds move the intended measure.** Halving and doubling the action rate gives 668 and 1,822
  completed actions against a training mean of 1,151, and moves the return-rate rule to 10.5% and 42.3%;
  doubling households doubles the household segment; doubling defect incidence gives six defect-prone styles.
  In world 2507, with all multipliers at 1, propensity does not depend on persona (chi-square 0.24 on 4 degrees
  of freedom, p = 0.99).

## Decision taken from this evidence

**AM-25: the style-targeting threshold is a linked-claim share of 0.075 with at least three linked claimants.**
Of the 40 hot-list style-worlds, 38 are targeted and 2 are sub-threshold. Now that honest linked claims exist,
untargeted styles reach a linked-claim share of 0.055 and up to four linked claimants.

| Linked-claim share threshold | Fired | Correct | Precision | Recall (95% interval) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 36 | 34 | 0.94 | 0.89 (0.76-0.96) |
| 0.075 | 33 | 33 | 1.00 | 0.87 (0.73-0.94) |
| 0.10 | 30 | 30 | 1.00 | 0.79 (0.64-0.89) |
| three linked claimants alone | 38 | 36 | 0.95 | 0.95 (0.83-0.99) |

0.075 is the lowest value tried that lets no untargeted style through. The intervals are wide: 38 targeted
styles cannot establish much, and the threshold was chosen on the same worlds it is reported on. The quality
threshold stays at 0.30: all 30 defect-prone style-worlds lie between 0.379 and 0.448 and no ordinary style
exceeds 0.051. That gap is wide by construction, so the quality signal is not a demonstrated capability.

## What remains different between honest and abusive requests

These are measured in the notebook's artefact audit and are left in deliberately.

1. **Order shape under a claim.** 33.8% of honest claims sit on single-line orders against 49.7% of solo and
   57.2% of ring claims. Abusers claim once per order whatever its size, while genuine failures scale with the
   number of units. None of the 18 features reads the size of the order under a claim.
2. **Recency.** Ring claims fall in the last 90 days more often (32.0% against 22.8%) and on lines not yet mature
   more often (13.2% against 8.4%), because rings act only once their members exist and the base grows.
3. **Withdrawn, late and multi-unit requests are honest only**, by specification.
4. **Outcomes.** 92.2% of honest requests are refunded against about 96.5% of abusive ones, which follows from
   item 3.

## Points to watch for the next stage

1. **The 75-85% model target may be out of reach.** The independent review reached 0.60-0.69 with a quick
   logistic regression on the earlier worlds. A third of abusers act only once and are close to undetectable.
   The decision is to wait for the real candidates and then either keep the target or report what is achieved.
2. **The household and defective-buyer legs of the gate are tight** (4.2% and 6.1% for the baseline).
3. **Missing evidence is informative.** Most honest eligible customers have no transaction claim at all; a
   missing-value indicator on claim features carries signal of its own.
4. **Honest repeat claimants.** Each world has roughly 350-410 honest customers with two or more genuine
   failures. This is what keeps claim counting weak, and it may be too many to be realistic.
5. **The fan-out cap rarely binds**: at most three address values per world exceed 25 accounts.
