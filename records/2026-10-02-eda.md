# Exploration record, 2026-10-02

Exploration of the development worlds under specification v1.4 (component 05). Source:
`notebooks/02_exploratory_data_analysis.ipynb`, with tables under `reports/eda/2026-10-02/`.

Worlds: fixtures 2001-2003 (1,000 customers), training 2101-2110 (10,000 customers) and stress 2501-2507.
All twenty validate against the data contract. **No validation, test or demo world existed when this was
measured.** Every number below comes from simulated data and says nothing about real customers. All worlds
share one generator and one configuration, so agreement between them shows stability, not generalisation.

## History of this record

This is the fourth set of worlds generated today, and the only one that still exists.

1. **First worlds (specification v1.1).** Showed that every customer was new (tenures of at most a year) and
   that most ring attempts failed because the member had not signed up. Fixed in v1.2 (AM-14, AM-15).
2. **Second worlds (v1.2).** Explored, then reviewed by an independent critic
   (`records/2026-10-02-eda-critic-review.md`). The review found that abusive requests could be recognised by

3. **Third worlds (v1.3).** Reviewed again (`records/2026-10-02-data-critic-re-review.md`). The earlier
   artefacts were gone, but the review found a new one (a ring claim always sat on the first line of its order)
   and three smaller ones (reason-code mix, whole-parcel claims, a step in signups). Fixed in v1.4 (AM-29).
4. **Fourth worlds (v1.4).** Described below.

## What the training worlds show (ten worlds pooled unless stated)

| Measure | Value |

| Abusers: all / eligible / excluded for thin history | 3,517 / 3,181 / 336 (9.6% of abusers) |
| Prevalence among eligible customers | 5.62% |
| Accounts with a propensity that act at least once | 54.1% |

| Return-rate rule, precision in the top 5% | 23.7% (671 of 2,836); 18.9-28.1% per world |
| Claim-count rule, precision in the top 5% | 42.5% (1,204 of 2,836); 39.0-45.4% per world |

- **Generation targets are met.** Claim, temporary-use and joint propensity are 4.0%, 3.1% and 0.6%; mean
  action rate 0.198; genuine fulfilment failures 1.5% of delivered units; carrier damage 0.5%. Two measures sit
  below target for a known reason: late honest returns near the cutoff fall after it, and Dormant accounts
  cannot receive the heavy-return overlay.
- **The return-rate rule is inside its 20-30% calibration band** without tuning.
- **Turning a propensity into an action depends on order count.** Among accounts with a propensity, 12% of those
  with no delivered order have acted, 55% of those with 3-5 orders and 92% of those with 21 or more.
- **Shortcuts.** On held-out training worlds, persona alone gives an AUC of 0.63 over all customers and 0.55
  among eligible customers; order count alone gives 0.72 and 0.56. The all-customer figures exceed the 0.60
  level at which the specification asks for an investigation: the cause is the accounts with no order (27% in
  the reference world), who can never be abusers. Among eligible customers neither shortcut is strong.
- **Evaluation segments are large enough.** Over five training worlds the benign segments hold 2,336 heavy
  returners, 5,406 defective-product buyers and 3,485 household members, against the 200 the gate requires.
- **What the two rules would do to the gate's segments.** The return-rate rule wrongly selects 44.2% of honest
  heavy returners, 6.0% of defective-product buyers and 3.9% of household members. The claim-count rule selects
  5.3%, 7.4% and 2.9%: it would fail the defective-buyer leg. A candidate model has to beat 3.9% on households
  and 6.0% on defective-product buyers, which are the tight legs.
- **Order volume.** Base orders rise from 48,538 in February to 63,996 in August (1.32 times) with the growing
  customer base; signups per month are flat.
- **Honest repeat claimants.** Each world has 359-433 honest customers with two or more genuine failures and
  177-210 with three or more, against 338-368 abusers. This is what keeps claim counting weak.
- **Stress worlds move the intended measure.** Halving and doubling the action rate gives 720 and 1,954
  completed actions against a training mean of 1,227, and moves the return-rate rule to 14.4% and 44.1%;
  doubling households doubles the household segment (1,392 against 698); doubling defect incidence gives six
  defect-prone styles. In world 2507, with all multipliers at 1, propensity does not depend on persona
  (chi-square 0.24 on 4 degrees of freedom, p = 0.99).

## The style-targeting threshold (AM-25), re-measured

AM-25 adopted a linked-claim share of 0.075 with at least three linked claimants on the v1.3 worlds. On the
regenerated worlds 39 of the 40 hot-list style-worlds are targeted.

| Linked-claim share threshold | Fired | Correct | Precision | Recall (95% interval) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 36 | 35 | 0.97 | 0.90 (0.76-0.96) |
| 0.075 | 34 | 34 | 1.00 | 0.87 (0.73-0.94) |
| 0.10 | 30 | 30 | 1.00 | 0.77 (0.62-0.87) |
| three linked claimants alone | 39 | 38 | 0.97 | 0.97 (0.87-1.00) |

0.075 is again the lowest value tried that lets no untargeted style through, so AM-25 stands. The second review
ran a leave-one-world-out check on the v1.3 worlds: choosing the lowest zero-false-fire threshold on nine worlds
picked 0.055 every time and was right on the held-out world, so 0.06 would do as well as 0.075, and the
three-claimant condition does most of the work. In stress world 2506 (action rate doubled) an untargeted style
reached 0.059, a margin of 0.016. The threshold is an engineering default with wide intervals, chosen on the
worlds it is reported on. The quality threshold stays at 0.30 (30 of 30 defect-prone style-worlds fire, none at
0.50); that gap is wide by construction, so the quality signal is not a demonstrated capability.

Of 638 customers with a linked claim, 62 (9.7%) are honest; the second review found every honest one was a
household member, so the link signal's honest noise comes from one mechanism.

## What remains different between honest and abusive requests

Measured in the notebook's artefact audit and left in deliberately.

1. **Order shape under a claim.** 33.9% of honest claims sit on single-line orders against 48.0% of solo and
   53.8% of ring claims. A solo abuser claims at most once per order, while genuine failures can strike several
   lines. No feature reads the size of the order under a claim.
2. **Recency.** Ring claims fall in the last 90 days more often (30.2% against 22.6%) and on lines not yet
   mature more often (12.0% against 8.3%). A ring acts only once two of its members exist, so ring claims grow
   from about 120 a month in October to about 230 in August, while honest claims follow order volume.
3. **Withdrawn and late requests are honest only**, by specification.
4. **Outcomes.** 92.2% of honest requests are refunded against 96.2-96.4% of abusive ones, which follows from
   item 3.
5. **Partial parcels and mixed orders** (third review, ten worlds). A non-receipt claim covering several lines of
   an order but not all of them: 41 solo and 1 ring against 11 honest, because a solo parcel claim takes only
   the units no honest request reserved. An order with a non-receipt claim and an item return on another line:
   376 abusive against 505 honest. Both are rare and read by no feature; the artefact audit now reports them.
6. **Campaign orders never contain two sizes of one style**, and honest requests on their other lines are
   slightly rarer than on base orders (second review: 17.5% against 20.6%, about 2.4 standard errors). Not
   visible to any feature; left as is.
